generated 2026-08-22 08:31 UTC from the files named at the end

THE QUESTION
  If no player ever submits an order holding both Mod A and Mod B, the corpus
  can never learn which loads first. A mod author who writes it down in their
  description is the only source of that constraint. So: read them all.

THE SHORT ANSWER
  Three usable constraints from the entire catalogue, at about 43 percent
  precision on the set that mattered. Nothing has been generated from this.
  The exercise was still worth it, because it identified the real bottleneck,
  which is not the prose.

THE SPEC THAT WAS USED
==============================================================================

  ACCEPT, 7 patterns. Each states which mod loads first, so a match
  becomes a directed edge or nothing.

    subject-after-named   [named-then-subject]
      means  : the described mod loads after the one it names
      example: "And this mod needs to be loaded after the Basket Full of Equipment mod."

    named-after-subject   [subject-then-named]
      means  : the mod it names loads after the described mod
      example: "that mod needs to loaded after this one."

    subject-before-named   [subject-then-named]
      means  : the described mod loads before the one it names
      example: "Load it before Unique Tav."

    named-before-subject   [named-then-subject]
      means  : the mod it names loads before the described mod
      example: "load those before this mod."

    imperative-after   [named-then-subject]
      means  : told plainly to load this one after the thing it names
      example: "Load this after the main race file."

    requires-loaded-first   [named-then-subject]
      means  : a requirement that also states order
      example: "This requires either the NSFW or SFW Basket Full of Equipment mod to be loaded first in the mod load order."

    absolute-end   [absolute]
      means  : the described mod belongs at one end, naming nobody
      example: "Place at the very bottom of your load order."

  REFUSE, 7 patterns. Refusal beats any accept. These do more work than
  the accepts do.
    manager-ui           instructions for the mod manager, not about mods
    comparison           names a mod as an analogy, not as a constraint
    savegame             about loading a save, not load order
    runtime              describes what the mod does at runtime
    pointer              points elsewhere instead of stating the constraint
    conditional-vague    a suggestion the author does not stand behind

  GUARDS
    - a statement with no named target never looks one up. A real mod is called
      "Load order - Cheese Collection", so its title stem is the words "load
      order" and it matched any sentence containing them.
    - a sentence matching two different mods is dropped. Ambiguity is not
      evidence, and picking the longest hides the coin toss.
    - minimum title length 8, longest match wins.
    - the author corroborates, never matches.
    - never an edge to itself.

==============================================================================
WHAT I WOULD DO WITH THIS
==============================================================================

  DO NOT ship any of it automatically. One of the seven inverts its direction,
  and an inverted edge reorders somebody game.

  The three good constraints are worth adding by hand as curated rules, with
  one caution: a curated rule naming a mod that only one order carries is
  exactly what took the submission pipeline down for fourteen hours on
  2026-08-20. That is fixed, but the shape of the risk is worth remembering.

  The join is the real bottleneck, and I do not have a fix for it. Sixty
  extracted claims are waiting on it, as is a share of the 803 requirement
  edges already in the catalogue. I thought the UUIDs in descriptions would
  open it and they do not: 70 new joins across the whole catalogue, which moves
  coverage by well under a point.

  So the honest position is that reading Nexus prose is close to exhausted,
  and the 71 percent of masterlist mods with no Nexus listing at all are
  where the answer would have to come from. Many are probably mod.io only,
  or unlisted, or renamed past recognition. That is a different question and
  it has not been asked yet.
